\begin{abstract}
Image generation provides dense supervision, yet its benefits for visual understanding remain unclear.
We ask: how does generation supervision improve visual understanding?
% \xd{should we just ask one question? lol better aligned with our title}
% controlled study
Using controlled pairs of image-to-image (I2I) and image-to-text (I2T) tasks that are two variants of the same puzzle, we find that \textit{only under an appropriate recipe} can I2I supervision substantially improve I2T performance, with \textit{gains increasing as more I2I data is added}.
% taskonomy
We next ask which kinds of generation data transfer to which understanding tasks.
To study transfer beyond paired tasks, we introduce \methodname, a unified taxonomy spanning 15 I2I supervision tasks and 25 I2T capabilities. The resulting transfer map reveals selective benefits: some follow intuitive correspondences, such as depth supervision improving metric 3D reasoning and object pointing improving counting, while others cross capability boundaries; e.g.~keypoint supervision improves lighting understanding.
% We find that transfer can follow intuitive correspondences, such as depth improving metric 3D reasoning and object pointing improving counting, but can also extend to less directly related capabilities, such as keypoints improving lighting understanding.
% The same generation task can also benefit some targets while interfering with others.
% gradient
To understand these transfer patterns, we analyze the gradient from generation and understanding objectives. 
In controlled task pairs, we show that I2I and I2T gradients align most in early pre-attention normalization layers.
Extending this analysis to \methodname, we find that capabilities with higher gradient alignment at these layers also exhibit larger downstream transfer gains.
Together, our results show that image generation can provide effective training-time supervision for visual understanding, with benefits that depend strongly on the training recipe, the amount of direct understanding supervision, and the source–target task pairing.
\end{abstract}
