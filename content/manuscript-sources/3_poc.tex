\section{Does image generation help visual understanding?}
We first study whether image generation can improve visual understanding at all.

Studying this requires a clean setting to test whether knowledge learned from generation tasks can transfer to understanding tasks.

We therefore construct controlled tasks in which the generation and understanding objectives require solving essentially the same visual task, differing primarily in the required output modality. 
This allows us to examine whether the generation objective itself provides useful supervision for visual understanding.
Our controlled framework is model-agnostic and applies in principle to any unified model with both generation and understanding capabilities.
We instantiate it on BAGEL, a unified multimodal model based on a
Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) architecture.

Under this setup, we investigate three questions.
First, can I2I or T2I supervision improve performance on the corresponding understanding task?
Second, how should generation and understanding data be combined during training?
Third, how does transfer depend on the relative data budgets of the two objectives?

\subsection{Controlled Generation-to-Understanding Transfer Setup}
We construct paired generation and understanding tasks that require the same visual operation but produce answers in different modalities. 
In all experiments, transfer is measured by performance on the corresponding I2T task.

In Jigsaw, patches of an image are shuffled. 
In Zoom-In, five views of the same image at different zoom levels are shuffled. 
For both tasks, the I2I objective generates the correctly ordered image, while the paired I2T objective predicts the correct permutation in text, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} (a).
The two objectives therefore solve the same task from the same input, differing only in the output modality.

\subsection{How Should Generation and Understanding Be Combined?}
% show the scaling curve of I2I toy tasks under different recipes;
% show the curve udner different I2T data budgeste, enough I2I can appriximate I2T on toy settings
We next study how I2I and I2T supervision should be combined during training.
We compare an I2T-only baseline (R1) with five training recipes:
(R2) I2I pretraining followed by I2T finetuning, with the shared understanding weights updated during I2I training;
(R3) mixed I2I-I2T training followed by I2T finetuning;
(R4) I2I pretraining with the understanding weights frozen, followed by mixed training;
(R5) mixed training throughout; and
(R6) I2I pretraining with the understanding weights updated, followed by mixed training.
To identify the best recipe, we fix the I2T data budget and vary the amount of I2I data. 
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:scaling} (b), R2 and R6 scales consistently as the I2I data increases. 
Both recipes first train on I2I data to update the understanding branch gradients. 
In contrast, freezing these weights or mixing the two objectives from the beginning leads to weaker or less stable gains. 
We therefore use I2I pretraining followed by I2T finetuning as the default recipe in subsequent experiments.
\begin{findingbox}
I2I Training through weight sharing provides a good initialization.
\end{findingbox}

\subsection{How Does Transfer Vary Across Data Regimes and Instances?}
We next ask whether I2I supervision can substitute for direct I2T supervision.
We fix the I2I budget at 100k examples and vary the I2T budget from 1k, 3k, 10k, and 30k, comparing with training on I2T-only data.
As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}(c), performance continues to improve as more I2T data is added, indicating that I2I supervision complements but does not fully replace direct understanding supervision.
Its benefit is largest when the I2T budget is small.

We further examine whether generation and understanding success are aligned
at the instance level.
As shown in Table~\ref{tab:instance_alignment}, I2T accuracy is substantially
higher when the corresponding I2I prediction is correct, with gaps of
17.9 points on Jigsaw and 14.8 points on Zoom-In.
This suggests that the paired objectives exhibit substantial instance-level
alignment, although shared example difficulty may partly contribute to this effect.
