Existing benchmarks organize visual tasks primarily by task formulation or output modality, resulting in largely separate taxonomies for understanding and generation.

This makes it difficult to determine how seemingly different tasks relate in terms of the visual information they require.
We introduce \methodname, a shared hierarchy that organizes I2I supervision objectives and I2T evaluation capabilities according to the visual information they target, rather than their output modality.
\methodname follows a two-level structure.
At the top level, we adopt the three foundational problems of computer vision~\citep{malik2016three}: \textit{Recognition}, \textit{Reconstruction}, and
\textit{Reorganization}.
Within these three families, we derive a set of fine-grained capability from existing visual tasks and benchmarks.
We then map both I2I objectives and I2T benchmark samples into this unified capability space.
Full construction details, capability definitions, and annotation protocols are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:unitaskonomy}.

\paragraph{Organizing visual capabilities with the 3Rs.}
We use the three Rs of computer vision~\citep{malik2016three} as the top-level structure of \methodname.
\textbf{Recognition} concerns identifying and assigning semantic meaning to visual content, including object and part identity, appearance and attributes, state, activity, text, and symbolic content.
\textbf{Reconstruction} concerns recovering geometric and photometric properties of the observed scene or its image-plane projection, including 2D spatial arrangement, depth, distance and metric scale, orientation, shape and curvature, lighting, and occluded geometry.
\textbf{Reorganization} concerns structuring visual elements into coherent groups and correspondences, including localization, grouping and segmentation, correspondence, ordering, counting, keypoints, and connectivity.
Importantly, these categories characterize \emph{what visual information must be recovered or organized}, rather than the modality in which the answer is expressed, making them a natural scaffold for relating I2I and I2T tasks.

\paragraph{Discovering fine-grained capabilities from diverse visual tasks.}
We use existing visual understanding benchmarks as the primary source for discovering fine-grained capabilities as they span a broad set of perceptual and reasoning requirements.
% benchmark selection
We first collect samples from widely used vision-language benchmarks that cover diverse visual capabilities, including BLINK~\citep{fu2024blink}, MMStar~\citep{chen2024wemmstar}, MMT-Bench~\citep{ying2024mmt}, CV-Bench~\citep{tong2024cambrian}, RealWorldQA~\citep{xai2024realworldqa}, MMVP~\citep{tong2024eyes}, and VStarBench~\citep{wu2024vstarbench}.
% attribute annotation
Then, for each sample, we prompt a Vision-Language Model (VLM) to generate dense, free-form descriptions of all visual capabilities needed to solve the task.
For example, a sample might yield descriptors like \emph{relative depth}, \emph{size comparison}, and \emph{occlusion}.
This generates a dense set of descriptive attributes per sample.
We then use an LLM to organize the pooled descriptors within the 3R hierarchy, providing an initial grouping of semantically related concepts.
For example, descriptors concerning whether one object lies in front of another or the ordering of objects along the viewing direction are grouped into a candidate \textit{Depth Understanding} capability under \textit{Reconstruction}.
Final coverage and capability boundaries are documented in
Appendix~\ref{app:unitaskonomy-induction}.

\paragraph{Consolidating the capabilities.}
The LLM-induced groups serve as candidate capability clusters rather than the final taxonomy.
We subsequently review and consolidate these groups.
Specifically, we merge synonymous or redundant groups, split groups that conflate distinct visual capabilities, reassign groups whose placement is inconsistent with the 3R definitions, and remove descriptors that characterize task domain or difficulty rather than visual capability itself.
This LLM-assisted, human-finalized process yields the leaf-level capability vocabulary shown in \autoref{fig:taskonomy}. The final taxonomy and its scope are documented in Appendix~\ref{app:unitaskonomy-consolidation}.
\paragraph{Integrating I2I tasks.}
We next incorporate I2I objectives into the same 3R hierarchy.
We collect image-to-image and dense visual prediction tasks, including those formalized in Taskonomy~\citep{zamir2018taskonomy}, and identify the primary visual information directly supervised by each prediction objective.
Each I2I objective is represented as its own typed leaf and placed under the appropriate 3R family.
For example, Z-depth and Euclidean-depth prediction are placed under \textit{Reconstruction} alongside the I2T \textit{Depth Understanding} capability, while 2D keypoint prediction is placed under \textit{Reorganization} alongside the I2T \textit{2D Keypoints} capability.
The resulting taxonomy contains 15 I2I supervision leaves and 25 I2T capability leaves, with leaf type explicitly indicating whether a node represents a training objective or an evaluation capability.
This yields a common visual hierarchy in which generative and understanding tasks can be compared directly without asserting task equivalence.
Appendix~\ref{app:unitaskonomy-i2i} provides the complete I2I task inventory and mappings.

\paragraph{Annotating Benchmark Samples}
% 把每个sample填进去， 多个llm judge majority vote， 计算human correlation
Once the capability vocabulary is finalized, we separately assign each I2T benchmark sample to its primary capability leaf.
To scale this annotation, three independent LLM judges label each sample according to the finalized taxonomy and annotation guidelines, and the final label is determined by majority vote.
A sample is retained when at least two of the three judges agree on its label, and samples without a two-of-three consensus are excluded.
The retained-label agreement, exclusions, and representative examples are provided in Appendix~\ref{app:unitaskonomy-annotation}; all capability definitions are listed in Appendix~\ref{app:unitaskonomy-definitions}.
