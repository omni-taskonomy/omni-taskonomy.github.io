01

Does image generation help visual understanding?

To isolate the effect of output modality, we construct paired I2I and I2T tasks that require solving the same task from the same input, differing primarily in whether the answer is produced as an image or as text.

in Jigsaw, image patches are shuffled, and in Zoom-In, five views of the same image at different zoom levels are shuffled.

For both tasks, the I2I objective generates the correctly ordered image, while the corresponding I2T objective predicts the correct permutation in text.

02

Which Training Recipe Transfers Best?

We compare an I2T-only baseline (R1) against five training recipes:

1
  1. R1: I2T only.

    Train only on I2T.

  2. R2: I2I → I2T.

    Pretrain on I2I while updating the shared understanding weights, then finetune on I2T.

  3. R3: Mixed → I2T.

    Jointly train on I2I and I2T, then finetune on I2T.

  4. R4: Frozen I2I → Mixed.

    Pretrain on I2I with the shared understanding weights frozen, then switch to mixed I2I–I2T training.

  5. R5: Mixed only.

    Jointly train on I2I and I2T throughout training.

  6. R6: I2I → Mixed.

    Pretrain on I2I while updating the shared understanding weights, then switch to mixed I2I–I2T training.

We therefore use I2I training followed by I2T finetuning as the default recipe in subsequent experiments.

I2I supervision complements but does not replace direct I2T supervision, with the largest gains in low-I2T settings.

03

OmniTaskonomy: A Unified Taxonomy of Visual Capabilities

We introduce OmniTaskonomy, a shared hierarchy that relates I2I tasks to understanding capabilities based on the visual information they require, rather than their output modality.

Annotation protocol

Once the capability is finalized, we assign each I2T benchmark sample to a capability leaf. To scale this annotation, we use three independent LLM judges to label each sample using the finalized taxonomy definitions and annotation guidelines. We keep a sample when at least two of the three judges agree on the same capability and use the majority label as its final assignment, and samples without a two-of-three consensus are excluded.

04

Which generation tasks help which understanding capabilities?

We now evaluate the 19 I2I tasks in OmniTaskonomy across its 25 understanding capabilities.

Useful generation supervision thus extends across recognition, geometry, and spatial reasoning, with different sources benefiting different capabilities.

Useful source-target pairs can share visual requirements.

Counting improves with all 19 sources, with 14 gains reaching p<0.05. Localization and object pointing yield the largest observed gains (+3.70 and +3.57 pp, respectively; both p<0.001): both objectives require identifying and locating objects.

Geometry supervision also benefits metric 3D relation: Z-depth, Euclidean depth, and surface normals improve accuracy by 2.90, 2.77, and 2.49 pp, respectively (all p<0.03).

Different sources benefit complementary capabilities.

Attribute editing improves category recognition (+2.81 pp, p<0.01), and object editing improves 2D ordering (+8.42 pp, p<0.03). Inpainting and 2.5D segmentation also improve ordering (+9.47 and +11.58 pp), while colorization gives the largest observed gain on visual correspondence (+5.71 pp; all three p<0.01).

05

What Explains Generation-to-Understanding Transfer?

We sample 500 matched source pairs and compute the corresponding I2I and I2T gradients at the pretrained checkpoint.

We observe a strong positive correlation (r=0.953): capabilities whose gradients are more aligned with I2I objectives on average also receive larger average gains from I2I training.

Alignment and transfer are positively correlated across these 105 pairs (r=0.496).

I2I and I2T gradients align most strongly in early pre-attention normalization layers. Across these seven capabilities, higher average alignment is associated with larger average transfer gains.
